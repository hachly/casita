# Casita privacy policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

Casita keeps photos, videos, files and web logins in encrypted spaces on your iPhone. This policy says exactly what Casita stores, what leaves your iPhone, and what we, the developer, can see.

## What stays on your iPhone

- Everything you add to Casita is encrypted on your iPhone with AES-256. On the iPhone, every guess at a code has to go through its Secure Enclave, so guessing can only happen on that iPhone.
- The exception: your recovery sentence together with a backup lets someone try codes on a computer. Keep the sentence private.
- Your codes and your recovery sentence are never stored, by Casita or by us. Casita checks a code by trying to unlock with it.
- Casita has no account and no sign-in.

## What Casita never does

- No analytics after setup (see Purchases below), no ads, no tracking, no data brokers.
- No reading your Photos library. Casita uses Apple's photo picker, which hands over only the photos you choose. Casita asks for full Photos access only if you tap "Delete originals", and only to delete those originals.
- No location. Photos taken with Casita's camera carry no location.
- No sending your photos anywhere to be sorted. Casita groups photos by date, place, people and things on your iPhone, with Apple's on-device tools and a list of towns built into the app. What it works out is stored inside the space, encrypted. Place names come from GeoNames (geonames.org), licensed under CC BY 4.0.

## What leaves your iPhone, and only then

- **Backups, if you turn them on.** A backup is the whole of Casita's storage, encrypted so that only your recovery sentence opens it. It goes to your own iCloud Drive, or wherever you choose to save it. We never receive it.
- **Purchases.** Payment is handled by Apple. Each time Casita opens, it asks RevenueCat whether you have a plan. RevenueCat receives your purchase records, a random ID created on your iPhone, the device identifier iOS gives our apps (the same for every app from us, different for every other developer), your iPhone's model and iOS version, language and region, App Store country, and your IP address. During setup, Casita also tells RevenueCat the furthest setup step you reached (for example "private code" or "paywall"), so we can see where setup is unclear; nothing about what you do after setup is sent. It never receives your name, email, or anything inside Casita. See RevenueCat's privacy policy at https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy.
- **Web.** Sites you open in Casita's browser see your visit as they would in any browser. Text typed in the address bar that isn't a web address is sent to DuckDuckGo as a search. Your logins are stored inside your space, encrypted, not by those sites' apps.
- **Ratings.** Casita may ask iOS to show the App Store rating prompt; iOS decides whether it appears.

## What we can see

Nothing inside your spaces, and not how many spaces you have. We cannot recover a forgotten code or a lost recovery sentence, and we cannot open a backup.

## Your choices

- Turn backups off, or delete a backup from iCloud Drive at any time.
- Delete a space from its own Settings or from the space above it.
- Delete Casita to erase everything on the iPhone. Backups you saved elsewhere stay until you delete them.
- Manage or cancel a subscription in your iPhone's Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions.

## Children

Casita is for adults. Where the law asks, Casita uses Apple's age range check, which shares only an age range, never a birthday.
